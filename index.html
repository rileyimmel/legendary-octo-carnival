You land around 7am. Metro from CPH airport into the city is ~15 min. Drop bags at Axel Guldsmeden in Vesterbro — they'll hold them until check-in (~3pm).

Morning

Walk or cab to Hart Bageri in Frederiksberg for breakfast — widely considered the best bakery in Copenhagen. Get the cardamom bun (kardemommesnurrer) and a tebirke (poppy-seed pastry with marzipan).

Then walk to the Botanical Garden + Rosenborg Castle + The King's Garden cluster. Tour Rosenborg's interior; the crown jewels in the basement are worth it. Plan 2–3 hours total.

Lunch

Atelier September — Nordic open-faced sandwiches and salads, very vegetarian-friendly. Tiny but worth the wait.

Alternative: Apollo Bar at Charlottenborg — modern Nordic with strong veg dishes.

Afternoon

Walk south through Strøget to Nyhavn. Take the canal cruise from Nyhavn (1 hour, Hey Captain or Stromma). After the cruise, walk the Amalienborg → Frederik's Church → Kastellet sequence — about 90 minutes with stops.

Evening

Check in, shower, then Tivoli Gardens for dinner and the evening. Restaurants: Grøften (classic Danish, veg smørrebrød), Mazzoli's Pizzeria, or Sticks'n'Sushi (huge veg menu). The gardens get magical after dark.

Day 3 — Louisiana + Christianshavn Sun, June 14

Morning

Quick bakery breakfast at Andersen & Maillard in Nørrebro (exceptional croissants). Train from København H to Humlebæk on the Coast Line (~37 min, every 20 min). Aim for the 10:00 or 10:20 train — Louisiana opens at 11am Sunday.

Late morning – afternoon

Louisiana Museum of Modern Art. Plan ~3 hours. Don't rush the Giacometti gallery (long room of bronzes facing the sea). The sculpture park has Calders, Henry Moores, and a path down toward the water with views to Sweden.

Lunch

Eat at the Louisiana cafe. The buffet is genuinely good — Nordic salads, roasted vegetables, soups, breads. The view over the Øresund is the entire point.

Afternoon

Train back to Copenhagen (~3pm). Metro or walk to Christianshavn. Climb the Church of Our Saviour (Vor Frelsers Kirke) — 400 steps, the last stretch is the external corkscrew on the spire. Then walk through Freetown Christiania (no photos in the marked sections).

Evening

Dinner at Mother in Kødbyen (Meatpacking District, walking distance from hotel) — Neapolitan pizza with serious veg options (the “Bufalina” is a classic). Alternative: Pony, upscale Nordic with a strong veg tasting menu.

Day 4 — CPH → Amsterdam Mon, June 15

Morning

Early breakfast — Hart, Andersen & Maillard, or Sankt Peders. Otherwise grab pastries at the airport. 9–9:40 flight, arrive Amsterdam ~10:30–11:30.

Train Schiphol → Amsterdam Centraal (~15 min), then to The Manor in Oosterpark. Drop bags.

Lunch

Head to the Jordaan. Eat at Winkel 43 for the famous Dutch apple pie. Or Vinnies Deli on Haarlemmerstraat for sit-down sandwich/salad lunch.

Afternoon

Walk Antiekcentrum Amsterdam on Elandsgracht — the antique mall, easily an hour. Cross the street to Vintage of Amsterdam. Walk the Nine Streets (De 9 Straatjes) for boutique shopping. Brouwersgracht is the prettiest canal — locals will tell you this. Swing by the Bloemenmarkt (floating flower market, open until ~5:30pm).

Bakery break: Petit Gateau near Westerstraat has miniature pies that are absurd.

Evening

Dinner at Hearth (near hotel, exceptional plant-forward menu) or Cafe de Klepel (small bistro, vegetarian set menu). For casual: Foodhallen indoor food court.

Day 5 — Dunes + Amsterdam Tue, June 16

Early morning

Bakery + coffee at Bakers & Roasters. Train Amsterdam Centraal → Overveen (~25 min via Haarlem).

Morning

Walk into Zuid-Kennemerland from Overveen entrance, take the dunes route west to Bloemendaal aan Zee on the coast — ~6–7 km, 2 hours moderate pace. Heath, pine, dune ridges, ending at the North Sea. Bus 81 from Bloemendaal → Haarlem station → train back. Total ~4 hours including transit.

Lunch

Back by ~1:30pm. Albert Cuyp Market in De Pijp — fresh stroopwafels off the iron, Surinamese roti (veg options available), Dutch cheese stalls. Open until 5pm. Sit-down alternative: Bataat in De Pijp (vegetarian/vegan).

Afternoon

Walk west from De Pijp to Vondelpark (~15 min) and stroll through. From the north end, walk into the Museum Quarter for the iconic “I amsterdam” view, then loop through the canal belt. Optional: Willet-Holthuysen (preserved 17th-century canal house, ~1 hour) or Hortus Botanicus (open until 5pm).

Evening

Dinner at The Pantry (traditional Dutch, solid vegetarian stamppot and fondue) or Hearth. Casual option: La Perla in Jordaan — best pizza in the city.

Morning

Check out. Rijksmuseum opens at 9am — go first thing. 90 min to 2 hours focused on highlights: Vermeer's Milkmaid, Rembrandt's Night Watch, the dollhouses, and the Asian Pavilion.

Late morning

Train to Utrecht (~30 min, every 10 min from Centraal). Drop bags at The Anthony.

Lunch

Theo Blom — one of the best bakeries in the country. Get a kruidkoek (Dutch spice cake) or appelflap. Or Hemel & Aarde for sit-down vegetarian-friendly lunch.

Afternoon

Utrecht is best on foot along the Oudegracht — canal with two-level wharves where former cellars are now bars and cafes. Walk it end to end. Climb the Dom Tower (book tour in advance — tallest church tower in NL, 465 steps). The Domkerk below is free.

Evening

Dinner at Karaf — natural wine and small plates, very vegetable-forward. Alternative: Cafe Olivier in a converted hidden Catholic church.

Notes!
Day 7 — Utrecht + Castle Thu, June 18

Morning

Bakery breakfast at Bakkerij Bond or De Kleinste Bakker (the smallest bakery in Utrecht). Then Rietveld Schröder House (UNESCO, book in advance, timed tours). Optional: Museum Speelklok — mechanical music boxes and street organs.

Lunch

Sandwich-and-pastry lunch at Bakkerij Roodenburg or grab from a market stall. Quick — the afternoon is the big event.

Afternoon — Castle

Kasteel de Haar in Haarzuilens — largest castle in the Netherlands, restored by the Cuypers family. Bus 111 from Utrecht Centraal (~30 min). Allow 2.5–3 hours for interior tour + gardens. The gardens alone are worth the trip.

Evening

Back to Utrecht. Anna (strong vegetarian menu), or Florin for something more substantial. Karaf holds up to a second visit.

Morning

Check out of The Anthony. Bags in lockers at Utrecht Centraal (or take to The Hague — Den Haag Centraal also has lockers). Train Utrecht → Den Haag Centraal (~40 min).

Walk to the Mauritshuis (~10 min from station) — Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring, Fabritius's Goldfinch, Rembrandt's Anatomy Lesson. 90 minutes. Stroll the Binnenhof next door.

Lunch

Anne&Max on Denneweg (very vegetarian-friendly) or Lola Bikes & Coffee for casual cafe lunch. Save room for pastries from Hartendief or De Banketbakker in the afternoon.

Afternoon

Escher in Het Paleis (former winter palace, Escher's tessellations and impossible architectures, 5-min walk from Mauritshuis). Then Panorama Mesdag — the giant 360° painting from 1881, ~45 min.

Optional: Tram to Scheveningen beach for an hour on the pier and sand.

Evening

Train Den Haag → Schiphol (~35 min direct). Hyatt Place is at the airport. Dinner at the hotel or in Schiphol Plaza — there's a vegetarian-friendly La Place in the airport.

9–9:40 flight, arrive Copenhagen ~10:30–11:30. Drop bags at the Clarion (right at the airport).

Bakery Crawl

Train into the city. A reasonable circuit:

  • Sankt Peders Bageri — oldest bakery in Copenhagen (1652), full lineup of traditional Danish pastries on Saturdays
  • Andersen & Maillard in Nørrebro — coffee and the croissant
  • Juno the Bakery in Østerbro — the cardamom bun
  • Mirabelle in Nørrebro — sourdough-focused, great savory rolls

Lunch

Final sit-down: Manfreds in Nørrebro — natural wine, vegetable-forward, lots of veg dishes.

Also a chance to walk a neighborhood you missed — Jægersborggade is a great street in Nørrebro, or Refshaaleøen (Reffen street food market, weekends only).

Evening

Train back to airport. Early dinner at the Clarion or in the area. Early bed.

Check out of Clarion by 12:00 and head to the terminal.
